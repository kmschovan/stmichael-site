Holy Days of Obligation

In addition to Sunday Mass, Catholics are obliged to attend Holy Mass on the following Holy Days of Obligation:

Holy DayDateWhat We Celebrate
Solemnity of Mary, the Holy Mother of God January 1 The Octave Day of Christmas, honoring the Blessed Virgin Mary as the Mother of God.
Solemnity of the Ascension of the Lord Thursday, the 40th day of Easter (movable) The Risen Christ ascends into heaven and is seated at the right hand of the Father. In the Ordinariate it is kept on its traditional Thursday.
Solemnity of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary August 15 At the end of her earthly life, Mary was taken up body and soul into heavenly glory.
Solemnity of All Saints November 1 The Church honors all the saints in heaven — those known by name and the countless unnamed.
Solemnity of the Immaculate Conception December 8 God preserved the Blessed Virgin Mary from all stain of original sin from the first moment of her conception. She is the Patroness of the United States.
Solemnity of the Nativity of Our Lord Jesus Christ December 25 Christmas — the Word became flesh and dwelt among us.

Why We Keep Them — From the Heart

Catholics attend Holy Days of Obligation to worship God together, receive the Eucharist, and mark the great events and mysteries of salvation history. Beyond a church requirement, these days offer regular spiritual nourishment and a chance to respond to God’s grace in love.

  • ✦Encounter Christ in the Eucharist. Mass provides spiritual nourishment through Scripture and Holy Communion, helping believers stay rooted in Christ rather than relying solely on private prayer.
  • ✦Honor the key mysteries of faith. Holy days spotlight the great moments involving Jesus, Mary, and the saints — Christmas, the Immaculate Conception, the Assumption, All Saints’ Day.
  • ✦Practice the virtue of religion. Giving God due worship and thanksgiving is a core moral act that puts love of God and neighbor first.
  • ✦Build communal faith. Worshipping alongside fellow Catholics unites the community and strengthens our shared faith.
  • ✦Fulfill a precept of the Church. As Ascension and Catholic Answers remind us, these obligations set an indispensable baseline for the life of prayer; missing Mass without a serious reason (such as illness) is considered a grave sin.

Here is how the six Holy Days fall in the coming liturgical year, and when Mass is offered at Saint Michael's for each:

What the Obligation Means

Attending Holy Mass on Sundays and Holy Days of Obligation is one of the precepts of the Church — the minimum asked of every Catholic. On those days the faithful are bound to participate in the Mass just as on Sunday, unless prevented by serious reason or dispensed by their pastor.

Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2180

The Holy Days in Our Ordinariate

Upon the recommendation of the Governing Council in 2016, Bishop Steven J. Lopes decreed that the six solemnities listed above are observed as Holy Days of Obligation throughout the Personal Ordinariate of the Chair of Saint Peter. Two customs are particular to us: the Ascension is kept on its traditional date, the Thursday forty days after Easter, and whenever January 1, August 15, or November 1 falls on a Saturday or a Monday, the obligation to attend Mass is abrogated — the feast is still joyfully celebrated, but attendance is no longer required.

Holy Day Masses at Saint Michael's

When a Holy Day falls on a weekday, Holy Mass is ordinarily offered at 6:30 pm; when it falls on a Sunday, the Sunday Masses of that day fulfill the observance.
